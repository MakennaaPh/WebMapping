About the HOLC and Redlining Layers

This map uses historic Home Owners' Loan Corporation (HOLC) residential security grades to show how neighborhoods in Beaumont were categorized by lending risk during the redlining era. Areas marked A or B were considered more desirable by lenders, while C and D areas were labeled as declining or hazardous, often reflecting racial discrimination, segregation, and unequal investment rather than actual neighborhood value.

The HOLC/redlining data used in this project comes from the University of Richmond's Mapping Inequality: Redlining in New Deal America project. Their Digital Scholarship Lab digitized and published historical HOLC maps and area descriptions, making it possible to compare these past lending categories with present-day demographic patterns and historically Black neighborhoods.

Historically Black Neighborhoods in Beaumont, Texas

By Makenna Phillips

This map explores how Black neighborhoods and historic HOLC grades appear across Beaumont’s urban landscape. The goal is to compare racial geography, historic lending risk categories, and present-day demographic patterns.

Project focus

Focus areas: Charlton-Pollard, Pear Orchard, and northern Beaumont.

Census layer: 2020 NHGIS block-group center points for Jefferson County, symbolized by percent Black population.
